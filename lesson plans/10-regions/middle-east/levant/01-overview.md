## Core material

Levantine Folk Music spans oud, voice, mijwiz leading voices, dabke, song forms, and line dance.

Study **levantine folk music** through listening first. Wikipedia's article on [Music of the Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_the_Levant) orients geography and history; recordings prove how musicians phrase ornaments, tempo, and ensemble balance in real rooms.

### Regional accent and social context

Tradition lives in **community practice** — festivals, dances, teaching networks, and archives — not only on concert stages. For **Middle Eastern & Mediterranean Folk**, ask who leads (voice, fiddle, pipes, percussion), whether dance drives repertoire, and how revival institutions shaped what you hear on modern recordings.

### Instruments and voices in this lesson

Lead voices: **oud, voice, mijwiz**. Ensemble layer: **percussion, ensemble**. Compare timbre and role with the anchor unit before assuming techniques transfer unchanged.

### Forms, dance, and rhythm

Named forms include **dabke, song forms**. Dance context: **line dance**. Metre and foot-tap feel are not decorative — they explain why musicians accent certain beats and phrase repeats.

### History, transmission, and today

Historical threads: **Levantine archives**. Contemporary scenes: **festivals**. Tune repertoire anchor: **Levantine repertoire**. Follow lesson playlists and reading lists for recorded examples.
