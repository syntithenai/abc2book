# Irish Tunes — Forms, History, and Recordings

**Track:** regions | **Tier:** 4.5 | **Difficulty:** 5/10

## Overview

This lesson turns the tune-type field guide into **named repertoire**. You will meet representative reels, jigs, hornpipes, polkas, slides, slow airs, and a planxty — with oldest references, players who popularised each tune, regional variants, and multiple YouTube examples in different styles.

Listen comparatively: the same title can sound like a Sligo roll-heavy reel in one hands and a spare Donegal reading in another. That variation is the tradition working.

## The Silver Spear

[[entity:silver-spear]] is among the most played reels in sessions worldwide. Francis O'Neill included it in *Music of Ireland*; [[entity:coleman]]'s New York 78s helped fix a setting that many players still treat as a reference. [[entity:hayes]] and other Clare fiddlers often approach the tune with more space and dynamic breathing.

Compare recordings below — notice bowing, ornament density, and tempo relative to dance.

## The Kesh Jig

*The Kesh Jig* appears in Brendan Breathnach's *Ceol Rince na hÉireann* and has become a learner's gateway into 6/8 repertoire. It chains easily with *The Merry Blacksmith* and other D-major jigs in pub sets.
