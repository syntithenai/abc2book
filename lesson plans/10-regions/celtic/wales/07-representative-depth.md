## Overview

This lesson maps **how Welsh traditional music left the chapel, the twmpath hall, and the eisteddfod stage** to meet rock amplifiers, festival crowds, diaspora choirs, and Breton collaboration. It assumes you know chapel history, industrial south transmission, and the 1970s revival (Lesson 6). Here the focus is **named bands, festival scenes, and choral crossover** from the 1980s to the present.

Think of Welsh music as braided strands: **male voice choir** hwyl, **folk club** acoustic tradition, **National Eisteddfod** prestige, and **folk-rock** festival energy.  — listen for bilingual lyrics and rock rhythm section beneath fiddle and harp lines. [[entity:bob-delyn]] knot them together for international listeners.

## Bob Delyn a'r Ebillion

[[entity:bob-delyn]] (Welsh: "Every Harp and the Pegs") is a **folk-rock** band formed **1988**, fronted by **Twm Morys** — poet, *Prifardd* (Chief Bard), and son of writer **John Morris-Jones**. The group sings in **Welsh and Breton**, blending trad tunes with **rock, reggae, country**, and roots arrangements.

### Formation and musical approach

Lineup has included fiddle, harp, guitar, bass, drums, and brass — **acoustic trad** meets **amplified festival** staging. Albums on **Sain** and **Recordiau Bos** (*Dal i 'Red*, *Surfio*, *Ymlaen*) feature dance tunes, political Welsh-language songs, and cross-Celtic collaboration (e.g. *Meur a Wech* — Welsh and Breton comparison).

### Landmark role

Bob Delyn is a **major figure in the 1990s Welsh folk revival** — proof that alawon gwerin could fill tents without losing language pride. Twm Morys's literary stature links **eisteddfod bardic culture** to **rock audience** — rare double credential.

**Memorable detail:** Band name puns on **every harp** in Wales — ambition as joke and mission.

## Festival culture

Welsh festival culture layers **competitive eisteddfod**, **folk weekenders**, and **inter-Celtic** events.

### National Eisteddfod

[[entity:eisteddfod]] moves annually between north and south; **maes** (field) hosts folk concerts, twmpath, harp stages, and rock tents. Winning folk categories launches careers; **evening concerts** mix choir hwyl with Bob Delyn-scale bands.

### Folk and world music festivals

**Green Man Festival** (Brecon Beacons) books Welsh folk alongside indie — crossover audience. **Sesiwn Fawr Dolgellau** and **Gwyl Gregynog** feature trad artists. **Interceltic Festival of Lorient** (Brittany) showcases Welsh acts in pan-Celtic context — Bob Delyn's Breton tracks shine here.

### Trac Cymru and touring

[[entity:trac-cymru]] funds **Welsh Folk Awards**, touring networks, and education — infrastructure Ireland mirrors through Comhaltas but with **smaller scale**, **bilingual mandate**.

## Choral crossover

**Choral crossover** connects folk melody to **mass participation**:

- **Male voice choirs** (*Treorchy*, *Morriston Orpheus*) perform hymn and folk arrangements in halls and on television
- **Only Boys Aloud** and youth choirs modernise image
- **Cymanfa Ganu** events draw thousands — folk singers join hymn hwyl
- Rugby internationals feature *Hen Wlad Fy Nhadau* and *Cwm Rhondda* — folk-hymn identity at stadium scale

[[entity:male-voice-choir]] tradition is not separate from folk — same valleys, same families, different Saturday night.

**Memorable detail:** A folk harpist and a choir bass may share a **eisteddfod coach** as teenagers — scenes interpenetrate.

## Legacy: porous boundaries

Session purists sometimes dismiss folk-rock; choral traditionalists sometimes dismiss twmpath. Living tradition **ignores** both complaints:

- Bob Delyn fans discover triple harp
- Choir singers join folk clubs
- Eisteddfod winners play Green Man

**Memorable detail:** Almost every fusion musician here also plays **straight trad** somewhere — boundary is porous, not walled.

## Key points

- **Bob Delyn a'r Ebillion** anchor 1990s Welsh folk-rock with Twm Morys as Chief Bard frontman.
- **National Eisteddfod** and folk festivals are dual prestige and popular stages.
- **Choral crossover** links hymn hwyl, male voice choirs, and folk melody at mass scale.
- Bilingual and **Breton** collaboration mark Welsh festival internationalism.
- Compared with Ireland: choral stadium culture and eisteddfod over Riverdance model.
- Trac Cymru and Sain sustain touring and recording infrastructure.
