# Welsh Traditional Music — Tunes, Forms, and Style

**Track:** regions | **Tier:** 4 | **Difficulty:** 6/10
**Prerequisites:** regions-celtic-wales-03-instruments-voices-ii
**Tags:** wales, plygi, hornpipe, waltz, hymn, penillion, ornament, bilingual

## Overview

If the instrument lessons taught you *who* speaks in a Welsh folk room, this lesson teaches you *what language they speak*. Welsh traditional music organises around **dance forms** — plygi, hornpipes, waltzes — alongside **hymn tunes** and **penillion** vocal art. Ornamentation turns a skeleton tune into a recognisable Welsh accent; bilingual titles and chapel harmony turn a single melody into a national symbol.

Take it in sections: learn to **identify the form** first, then study **penillion** as vocal genre, then explore **hymn tunes** as separate repertoire that sometimes crosses into twmpath sets.

## Compared with Ireland

Ireland's Genres lesson centres **reels, jigs, hornpipes, polkas, slides, sean-nós, and lilting**. Wales shares hornpipe and waltz metre with British neighbours but names **plygi** as distinct dance form and elevates **penillion** and **hymn tune** to genre status alongside instrumental dance music.

| Form | Ireland (`regions-celtic-ireland-04-genres-forms`) | Wales (this lesson) |
|------|--------------------------------------------------|---------------------|
| Primary dance drive | Reels and double jigs | **Plygi**, hornpipe, waltz |
| Signature vocal form | Sean-nós | **Penillion** over harp |
| Sacred crossover | Slow airs from song | **Hymn tunes** (*Cwm Rhondda*) |
| Mouth music | Lilting (*portaireacht*) | Penillion verse fitting |

## How to listen: metre, pulse, and hwyl

Train your ear for **metre** (beat grouping) and **hwyl** — the communal lift when voices and instruments surge together in hymn or folk song. Written time signatures guide but do not imprison: hornpipes may be notated 4/4 with dotted performance; penillion demands **strict fit** between harp bar and sung syllables.

**Foot-tap habit:** Plygi and hornpipe want **dotted bounce**; waltz wants **ONE-two-three**; hymn tunes may **stretch** phrase lengths for breathing in Cymanfa Ganu.

## Plygi

**Plygi** (plural *plygiau*) is a Welsh **folk dance form** — name related to "fold" or "bend" — often played as a **swinging 4/4** tune related to hornpipe repertoire. Plygi sets appear at **twmpathau** where callers teach figures between chorus lines.

### Feel and repertoire

Plygi **pulse** is dance-functional: accordion and fiddle articulate **long-short** patterns; guitar chords mark downbeats. Tunes may be local variants of wider British hornpipe stock or distinctly Welsh titles from collectors **Nicholas Bennett** (*Alawon Fy Ngwlad*) and later folk society editions.

**Memorable detail:** Dancers recognise plygi by **figure name** and **floor pattern** — musicians who only learn tune titles miss half the form.

---

## Hornpipes

**Hornpipes** in Wales share the **British Isles dotted 4/4** tradition — long-short swing, moderate tempo, bouncy footwork. Sailor associations and stage hornpipe dance echo Irish and English practice; Welsh settings may differ in **dotting** and **ornament** from Clare or Donegal readings.

### Session and twmpath role

Hornpipes chain in sets before waltzes or plygi at twmpath nights. Accordion leads often **signal** hornpipe sets with a characteristic intro figure. Compare recordings: Welsh folk club hornpipe may be **straighter** than Irish session dotting — regional taste, not error.

---

## Waltzes

**Waltzes** in 3/4 are twmpath staples — couple dances, circle waltzes, and newer choreographies from folk revival teachers. Melody may come from **imported** European waltz fashion (nineteenth century) or from **Welsh song airs** arranged for dance.

Guitar and accordion share **oom-pah-pah** or flowing arpeggio accompaniment; harp arrangements appear in eisteddfod repertoire. Waltz tempo must stay **dancer-safe** — faster is not better.

---

## Hymn tunes

**Hymn tunes** (*tune metrig* / *alawon emyn*) are a **genre category** in Welsh musical life, not merely church music. *Cwm Rhondda* (*Guide Me, O Thou Great Redeemer*), *Aberystwyth*, *Ton-y-Botel*, and hundreds of Welsh-composed melodies circulate in **Cymanfa Ganu**, male voice choir repertoire, and occasionally folk arrangements.

### Chapel DNA in folk

[[entity:male-voice-choir]] culture trained generations in **harmony**, **breath**, and **communal rhythm** — skills penillion singers and festival choirs reuse. A folk band may quote hymn tune fragments in medleys; a twmpath waltz may share harmonic language with Sunday chapel.

**Memorable detail:** *Cwm Rhondda* is among the world's most sung hymn tunes — Welsh composition (*John Hughes*, 1907) with global diaspora life.

## Penillion (canu penillion)

**Penillion** (*canu penillion* — "verse singing") is the art of composing or improvising a **counter-melody** in strict rhythm over a harpist's fixed tune. The singer's verse — often witty, topical, or praise poetry — must **fit the bar structure** without colliding with the harp melody.

### Technique and partnership

The harp plays an established **alaw** (tune); the singer enters with **penillion** lines that **answer** the melody. [[entity:penillion]] appears in eisteddfod competitions, folk club demonstrations, and teaching studios. It is **not** free-rhythm sean-nós — rhythmic discipline is the art.

### Historical and modern life

Medieval **bardic** praise poetry feeds penillion's prestige; modern harpists like **Llio Rhydderch** and teachers at eisteddfod workshops train new singers. Compared with Irish **lilting** (wordless mouth music), penillion is **texted verse** with harp partnership.

**Memorable detail:** A successful penillion performance leaves listeners unsure whether harp or voice "leads" — duet equality.

---

## Ornament and regional accent

Welsh instrumental ornament shares **cuts and rolls** with Irish fiddle vocabulary but applies them to **harp fingernail patterns** and **accordion bellows** differently. Harpists use **trills** and **grace figures**; fiddlers use **bowed trebles** sparingly compared to Sligo density.

Regional accent: **north Wales** harp repertoire emphasises penillion tunes; **south Wales** clubs may favour hornpipe–waltz sets with guitar harmony. Tag recordings by region when possible.

---

## Bilingual titles and song–tune overlap

Tunes carry **Welsh titles** (*Cader Idris*, *Dafydd y Garreg Wen*) with English exonyms in some books. Songs like [[entity:ar-lan-y-mor]] exist as **vocal airs** and **instrumental slow airs** — know which side of the repertoire you are on. Lesson 8 examines named tunes in depth.

## Key points

- **Plygi** is a Welsh dance form with hornpipe-family pulse and twmpath figures.
- **Hornpipes** and **waltzes** are core twmpath repertoire with dotted or triple feel.
- **Hymn tunes** are a Welsh genre category shaped by chapel and male voice choirs.
- **Penillion** is counter-melody singing over fixed harp tunes — rhythmic discipline is essential.
- Compared with Ireland: less reel-jig dominance; more hymn and penillion vocal genre weight.
- Metre and foot-tap must match dance; hwyl marks communal vocal lift.

**Answer:** A

### Q2. Plygi is:

- A) A Welsh folk dance form related to hornpipe repertoire
- B) A type of Irish reel in 6/8 only
- C) A Scottish bagpipe drone
- D) A jazz waltz exclusively

**Answer:** A

### Q3. Cwm Rhondda is best known as:

- A) A Welsh hymn tune sung worldwide
- B) A Breton bagpipe march only
- C) An electronic dance track
- D) A classical piano sonata exclusively

**Answer:** A

### Q4. Welsh hornpipes are typically in:

- A) 4/4 with dotted, bouncy feel
- B) 9/8 slip jig metre only
- C) Free rhythm without pulse
- D) 7/8 exclusively

**Answer:** A

### Q5. Compared with Irish sean-nós, penillion emphasises:

- A) Counter-melody over harp with strict bar fit
- B) Unaccompanied Irish-language song only
- C) Hard-shoe step dance exclusively
- D) Synthesizer leads only

**Answer:** A

### Q6. Waltzes at twmpath are usually in 3/4 time.

**Answer:** True

### Q7. Hymn tune repertoire is unrelated to Welsh folk identity.

**Answer:** False — hymnody deeply shapes Welsh musical culture.

**Answer:** False — regional accent and dance figures vary.

**Answer:** True

### Q11. Penillion singers may improvise topical verses.

**Answer:** True
