## Ar Lan y Môr (Beside the Sea)

[[entity:ar-lan-y-mor]] (*Beside the Sea*) is among the best-known **Welsh folk songs** — lyrical melody in **6/8** or related feel (settings vary), text evoking seaside love and loss. It circulates as:

- **Unaccompanied or guitar-led song** in folk clubs
- **Harp slow air** on triple harp
- **Choral arrangement** for male voice or mixed choir
- **Audience chorus** at festivals — hwyl moment

### History and transmission

The song entered **national consciousness** through twentieth-century print, radio, and eisteddfod folk song categories. Welsh lyrics are primary; English translations circulate for diaspora. Collectors placed it in **canu gwerin** (folk song) anthologies alongside hymn-influenced airs.

### Recordings to compare

Listen for **phrase breathing** (song), **ornament** (harp), and **tempo** (dance band instrumental settings are rarer but exist). Harpists like **Elinor Bennett** and **Llio Rhydderch** treat it as repertoire cornerstone; fiddle players adapt melody for twmpath **slow set** openings.

**Memorable detail:** *Ar Lan y Môr* is often a learner's **first Welsh song** — gateway to language and ornament.

## Cader Idris

[[entity:cader-idris]] names both **Snowdonia's mountain** (*Cadair Idris*) and a **folk tune** — harp and fiddle standard at eisteddfod and twmpath. Melody is energetic, **penillion-friendly**, and appears in **Nicholas Bennett**'s *Alawon Fy Ngwlad* lineage.

### Form and dance

Often played as **hornpipe or reel-family** dance tune depending on setting — musicians should agree **form** before starting. Accordion-fiddle bands drive twmpath sets; harp solo showcases **triple harp** technique in competition.

### Named recordings tradition

**Nansi Richards** ("Queen of the Harp") embodied *Cader Idris* in twentieth-century imagination. Contemporary **Patrick Rimes** (fiddle) and harpists refresh settings for festival stages. Compare two recordings: note **ornament density** and **tempo for feet**.

**Memorable detail:** Mountain and tune share name — landscape inscribed in repertoire.

## Learning strategy

1. Learn **one song** (*Ar Lan y Môr*) with lyrics and melody.
2. Learn **one dance tune** (*Cader Idris*) at twmpath tempo on your instrument.
3. Compare **harp and fiddle** recordings of *Cader Idris*.
4. Note **collector source** (Bennett, folk society edition) for your setting.

## Key points

- **Ar Lan y Môr** is a gateway Welsh song and harp slow air with festival chorus life.
- **Cader Idris** is a harp and fiddle dance standard linked to mountain identity.
- **Nansi Richards** anchors harp recording history; contemporary artists refresh settings.
- Nicholas Bennett's *Alawon Fy Ngwlad* is a key print source for tune names.
- Compare eisteddfod and twmpath readings for regional accent.
- Compared with Ireland: song-air centrality over session reel marathon.
