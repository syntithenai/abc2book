# Breton Traditional Music — Tunes — Forms, History, and Recordings

**Track:** regions | **Tier:** 4.5 | **Difficulty:** 5/10
**Prerequisites:** regions-celtic-brittany-04-genres-forms
**Tags:** brittany, suite-sudarmoricaine, gavotte, plinn, tunes, recordings

## Overview

This lesson turns Breton form theory into **named repertoire** — with emphasis on the **Suite Sudarmoricaine**, classic **gavotte** tunes heard at fest-noz, **plinn** standards from the Vannetais, and **bagad** arrangements that fixed export versions. Listen comparatively: the same gavotte title at a Quimper fest-noz, on a **Bagad Bro Kemperle** competition stage, and on a **Stivell** fusion album shares melody but differs in tempo, repeats, and arrangement mass.

Named tunes carry **dance contracts**: learn the melody with the figure family and region it serves. Mislearning a **Suite Sudarmoricaine** gavotte as abstract concert piece misses why repeats continue until dancers release the band.

## Compared with Ireland

Ireland's tune lesson centres session reels (*The Silver Spear*, *The Kesh Jig*) with O'Neill and Coleman histories. Brittany's parallel centres **gavotte** and **plinn** dance tunes with **Sonerion** books, **bagad** suites, and **Cornouaille** fest-noz programmes — fewer global session defaults, stronger regional naming in Breton and French.

## Suite Sudarmoricaine

The **Suite Sudarmoricaine** ("South Morbihan suite") aggregates **gavotte** and related dance tunes from **Cornouaille** and south Morbihan — a cornerstone of **bagad** repertoire and fest-noz veteran knowledge. It is not one tune but a **suite logic**: regional identity expressed through chained **gavottes** with distinct local names and figures.

[[entity:suite-sudarmoricaine]] appears on **bagad** competition programmes and in tune books as shorthand for Cornouaille export repertoire. **Bagad Bro Kemperle** and other bands recorded arrangements that learners treat as references — compare always to village fest-noz tempi.

### Listening approach

1. Identify individual gavotte titles within the suite on bagad recordings.
2. Note **bombard–binou** unison versus massed pipe harmonies.
3. Compare repeat length: fest-noz vs competition cut.

### Bombard–binou reference

Sonerion publications standardise melodies for **bombard** and [[entity:binou]] in B-flat; fiddle and accordion settings transpose for local bands. Learning from a single bagad YouTube clip without floor context risks wrong tempo.

## Plinn repertoire

**Plinn** tunes favour **steady 4/4** — examples on Vannetais accordion recordings and southeast fest-noz compilations. Titles may be place-based (*plinn from Gourin*, *plinn du pays pourlet*) tying melody to local walking figures.

[[entity:plinn]] programmes on fest-noz calendars often announce plinn blocks separately from gavotte — learn at least one plinn melody with **walking pulse** at sustainable tempo.

## Mazurka and son favourites

**Mazurka** tunes appear on mixed fest-noz nights — often French or Breton titles from nineteenth-century imports. **Son** repertoire links dance to **kan** chorus — *son ar chistr* (cider song) and regional *sonioù* appear on song-dance nights; melody doubles as dance pulse when choruses spread through the hall.

## Kan ha diskan sets (repertoire note)

While not a single tune, **kan ha diskan** **sets** — paired songs with overlapping lines — belong on learner playlists beside instrumentals. **Yann-Fanch Kemener** recordings document repertoire that fest-noz singers expect neighbours to know.

[[entity:kan-ha-diskan]] markers tie song sets to Lesson 4 form theory.

## Bagad competition sets beyond the suite

Bagad championships programme **marches**, **gavotte** arrangements, and **concert pieces** — e.g. material from **Bagad Kemper** repertoires recorded at Lorient. These are **transmission standards** for pipe and bombard sections even when not common on village floors.

[[entity:bagad]] recordings teach massed unison — distinct pedagogical tool from fest-noz dance leadership.

## Alan Stivell tune paths

[[entity:stivell]] rearranged Breton melodies — *Suite Sudarmoricaine* fragments, harp airs, binou lines — for fusion albums. Treat Stivell versions as **arrangement studies**: identify source dance form before importing concert tempi to fest-noz.

## Learning workflow

1. Pick one **gavotte** from Suite Sudarmoricaine family — learn A and B parts at fest-noz tempo on your instrument.
2. Attend or watch video of associated **dance figure** — connect repeat structure to floor.
3. Compare bagad arrangement — note added harmonies and cuts.
4. Add one **plinn** and one **mazurka** from Vannetais sources for form contrast.
5. Log variants in a **listening notebook** — tune name, region, leader instrument, tempo feel.

## ABC and print sources

- **Sonerion** tune books — standard bombard–binou settings.
- **Bagad** federation tune collections — competition material.
- Online ABC archives (Breton folk tune finders, regional sites) — verify against recordings.
- **Dastum** releases — village variants for historical comparison.

## Reading and listening list

- **Bagad Bro Kemperle** — Suite Sudarmoricaine and gavotte suite recordings.
- **Bagad Kemper** — championship sets from Lorient.
- **Alan Stivell** — fusion arrangements of Breton dance melodies.
- **Yves Menez** — Vannetais plinn and mazurka instrumental sets.
- **Dastum** — field gavottes compared to modern fest-noz.

## Key points

- Suite Sudarmoricaine names a Cornouaille gavotte suite logic, not one monolithic tune.
- Gavotte and plinn titles tie to regions and dance figures — learn melody with context.
- Bagad recordings fix export versions that may differ from village fest-noz practice.
- Stivell arrangements are arrangement layers atop dance repertoire.
- Compare recordings by scene: floor, bagad, fusion — before choosing a "standard."
