# Breton Traditional Instruments — Session Voices II

**Track:** regions | **Tier:** 3 | **Difficulty:** 5/10

**Tags:** brittany, accordion, guitar, bagad, fest-noz, vannetais

## Overview

This lesson covers **accordion**, **guitar**, and the **bagad** — three voices that shape Breton music beyond the bombard–binou pair. The **Vannetais** fest-noz floor often hears accordion-led **plinn** and **mazurka**; guitar supports **kan ha diskan** and modern fest-noz bands; **bagad** pipe bands train thousands of young musicians in a format with no Irish pub-session equivalent. Together with Lesson 2's winds, fiddle, and harp, you have the instrumental map of peninsula trad and its competition stages.

Accordion and guitar are sometimes dismissed as "modern additions" — historically inaccurate. Accordion entered rural Brittany in the nineteenth century and became **structural** to southeast dance culture. Guitar backs fusion concerts and informal **veillées**. Bagad is mid-twentieth century institutional innovation — but it now carries **gavotte suites**, **bombard** sections, and regional identity on par with any ancient pipe form.

## Compared with Ireland

Irish Session Voices II treats bodhrán, guitar, bouzouki, banjo, box accordion, and concertina as pub-session support and occasional lead. Breton accordion often **leads entire fest-noz sets** in the Vannetais; guitar is less central to dance pulse than Irish session backing but vital in **Bro Gozh** urban bands. **Bagad** has no Irish parallel in session culture — closest kin is Scottish pipe band, but Breton format adds **bombard** ranks and **Binou** sections with distinct competition repertoire.

| Voice | Irish session role | Breton fest-noz / bagad role |
|-------|-------------------|------------------------------|
| Accordion | Clare/Kerry box drive in sessions | Vannetais plinn/mazurka lead |
| Guitar | Chord backing, bouzouki patterns | Song sets, fusion bands, fest-noz support |
| Bagad | Not applicable | Massed pipes, bombards, drums — competitions |

## Accordion

The **diatonic accordion** (and related box layouts) drives much **Vannetais** fest-noz repertoire: **plinn**, **mazurka**, and some **gavotte** variants. Bellows direction changes articulation; players phrase for **walking dancers** maintaining steady pulse across long floors.

### Technique and cultural context

Vannetais accordion style favours **steady 4/4** and **3/4** dance rhythm — less dotted hornpipe swing, less reel acceleration. Ornament includes treble fills and bass pulse patterns that cue dancers' side-steps. Compared with Irish button box, Breton accordion repertoire aligns with **named dance figures** rather than arbitrary tune stacks.

### Fest-noz role

Accordion-led bands may omit bombard–binou entirely — not a "incomplete" trad lineup but a **regional contract**. Dancers from Cornouaille visiting Vannetais halls learn to follow accordion bounce instead of sonerion unison.

### Famous players and associations

**Yves Menez** and southeast fest-noz recordings document accordion-centred nights. Festival programmes at **Auray** and **Golfe du Morbihan** events showcase accordion–fiddle combinations.

## Guitar

Guitar in Brittany spans **folk backing**, **fusion** arrangements, and **song** accompaniment for **kan ha diskan**. It is rarely the sole dance-lead at classic northwest fest-noz but anchors **Bro Gozh** urban bands and post-Stivell fusion groups.

### Technique and cultural context

Rhythm guitar patterns fit **mazurka** and **gavotte** at moderate tempos; electric guitar enters rock-fusion and fest-noz rock crossover (e.g. **Fest-noz rock** movement echoes). Acoustic guitar supports vocalists in **veillées** where dance pauses for song.

### Fest-noz and concert role

In mixed fest-noz bands, guitar fills harmonic space when bombard–binou and fiddle carry melody. On fusion stages, guitar shares lead with harp and voice — different etiquette from village hall.

## Bagad

A **bagad** is a Breton pipe band: **binou** sections, **bombard** sections, **drums** (including **caisse claire** and **biniou braz** bass pipe in some formats), and competition **suites** combining parade drill with concert pieces. Wikipedia notes bagads arose in the mid-twentieth century as youth ensembles tied to Breton revival and regional rivalry.

### Structure and pedagogy

Bagads function as **music schools** — children learn pipes and bombards in ranked sections, march in uniform, and compete at **championships** drawing bands from across Brittany and diaspora branches. Repertoire includes **gavottes**, **marches**, and composed **suites** — including arrangements of **Suite Sudarmoricaine** material.

[[entity:bagad]] federations organise grades, adjudication, and festival showcases. **Bagad Kemper**, **Bagad Bro Kemperle**, and **Bagad Brieg** are reference names on recordings.

### Compared with Irish and Scottish pipe bands

Scottish pipe bands emphasise Highland pipes and snare drumming; Irish trad has no equivalent massed-youth pipeline in pub sessions. Breton bagad **requires** bombard sections and uses Breton tune forms — not a copy-paste of Scottish drill.

![Bagad performing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Bagad_Brieg.jpg?width=640)

### Fest-noz versus competition stage

Bagad concerts thrill festival audiences — **Festival Interceltique de Lorient** features major bagad finals. Village fest-noz may hire bagad subsets or smaller **sonerion** duos instead of full competition sets. Knowing which scale you are hearing prevents wrong expectations about floor figures.

## Reading and listening list

- **Bagad Kemper** — championship recordings for massed timbre and suite structure.
- **Yves Menez** — Vannetais accordion fest-noz repertoire.
- **Red Cardell** or **Bleizi Ruz** — guitar-forward fest-noz rock crossover (preview Lesson 7).
- **Fédération des bagadoù** materials — competition categories and grading.

## Key points

- Accordion leads much Vannetais fest-noz — plinn and mazurka pulse, not secondary "session backing."
- Guitar supports song, fusion, and mixed fest-noz bands; role varies by region and genre.
- Bagad is a distinct Breton institution — massed binou, bombard, and drums with youth pedagogy.
- Bagad competitions and village fest-noz answer different social needs from the same instrument pool.
- Compared with Ireland: no pub-session bagad equivalent; accordion regional lead differs from Clare session box.
