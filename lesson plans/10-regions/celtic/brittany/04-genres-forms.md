# Breton Traditional Music — Tunes, Forms, and Style

**Track:** regions | **Tier:** 4 | **Difficulty:** 6/10
**Prerequisites:** regions-celtic-brittany-03-instruments-voices-ii
**Tags:** brittany, gavotte, plinn, mazurka, son, kan-ha-diskan, ornament

## Overview

Breton dance music organises fest-noz life by **named forms** — each with metre, regional variant, choreography contract, and instrumental accent. Misidentifying a **plinn** for a **gavotte** is the Breton equivalent of confusing a reel with a jig: dancers feel the error instantly. This lesson maps **gavotte**, **plinn**, **mazurka**, **son**, and related **rond** dances; introduces **kan ha diskan** as vocal form; and explains ornament and phrasing on **bombard**, **binou**, and accordion.

Instrument lessons covered who leads; this lesson covers **what** they play and **how** pulse and melody behave. **Suite Sudarmoricaine** material — a cornerstone of Cornouaille **gavotte** repertoire — appears here as form example and returns in Lesson 8 with named tunes.


### Form listening tip

A **gavotte** is dance-first — foot patterns matter more than session tune order. **Kan ha diskan** is voice-led call-and-response, not instrumental reel trading.

## Compared with Ireland

Irish trad sorts session repertoire into reels, jigs, hornpipes, polkas, and slides — chained in **sets** by form. Breton fest-noz sorts into **dance types** with figures: **gavotte** couple and circle variants, **plinn** walking chains, **mazurka** triple-time steps, **son** dance-song hybrids. Irish **sean-nós** is solo song; Breton **kan ha diskan** is **duet dialogue** often on fest-noz programmes between instrumental sets.

| Form | Irish analogue (loose) | Breton distinction |
|------|------------------------|-------------------|
| Gavotte | Reel or polka drive (metre differs) | Named Cornouaille/Bro Léon figures |
| Plinn | Steady march or processional | Walking dance; Vannetais identity |
| Mazurka | Irish mazurka import | Fest-noz choreography distinct |
| Son | Lilting / song-dance | Fixed chorus + dance pulse |
| Kan ha diskan | Sean-nós (misleading) | Two-voice call-and-response |

## Gavotte

The **gavotte** is the signature Breton dance-tune family of **Cornouaille** and much of the west — steady quadruple feel, repertoire dense with local names, and export visibility through **bagad** suites and **Suite Sudarmoricaine** collections. Wikipedia's *Music of Brittany* links gavotte to fest-noz core repertoire alongside plinn.

### Metre and foot feel

Gavottes are commonly felt in **4/4** or regional variants with consistent beat for couple turns and circle **ronds**. Foot-tap: **ONE-two-three-four** with lift on choreography accents — not Irish reel **ONE-two THREE-four** swing. Tempo bands suit social dancers across age ranges; fest-noz bands slow when the floor fatigues.

### Regional variants

**Gavotte de l'Aven**, **gavotte pourlet**, and **gavotte des montagnes** name sub-styles tied to geography and figure sets. Cornouaille gavottes dominate tune books; Bro Léon shares overlapping repertoire with different fest-noz programmes.

[[entity:gavotte]] markers appear in tune metadata and ABC archives alongside dance instructions.

### Instrumental style

**Bombard–binou** unison carries melody with tongued ornament matching step accents. Fiddle doubles or alternates; accordion appears in mixed bands. **Bagad** arrangements add harmonic mass and percussion breaks unrelated to village hall pulse — compare field recordings before competition transcriptions.

## Plinn

**Plinn** (also **pliñ** in Breton contexts) is a **walking dance** in steady **4/4** — deceptively simple for musicians because tempo discipline must stay **dancer-neutral** for long chains. The **Vannetais** claims plinn as regional centrepiece; accordion-led bands often lead.

### Metre and foot feel

Dancers walk in lines, circles, or processional figures with minimal acrobatics — the challenge is **collective steadiness**, not flash. Musicians who accelerate to "energise" the room break plinn sociality.

### Repertoire notes

Plinn tunes may share melodic material with gavottes in some villages but are **programmed separately** on fest-noz bills. Callers announce plinn sets distinctly; switching form without announcement confuses the floor.

[[entity:plinn]] connects to southeast fest-noz programmes and tune books distinct from Cornouaille gavotte exports.

## Mazurka

Breton **mazurka** (3/4) arrived through nineteenth-century European dance fashion — parallel to Irish session mazurkas — but fest-noz choreography is Breton-specific: couple holds, turning patterns, and regional favourites on programmes from **Lorient** to **Gourin**.

### Metre and foot feel

Three beats per bar; emphasis patterns differ from waltz glide — listen for **side-close** step vocabulary on recordings with dance commentary. Accordion and fiddle share lead; bombard–binou sets include mazurkas in mixed nights.

## Son and rond dances

A **son** (plural **sonioù**) is a **dance-song**: verse and chorus sung while or between dance figures, linking **kan** traditions to instrumental pulse. **Rond** dances — **Rond de Saint-Vincent**, **Rond de Landeda**, **Rond de Saint-Malo** — are circle figures with named choreography tied to specific tunes.

### Kan ha diskan connection

Song forms bleed into dance night: a **son** chorus may spread through the hall; **kan ha diskan** pairs may perform between instrumental sets. [[entity:kan-ha-diskan]] is not isolated from **gavotte** nights — programmers weave voice and instrument.

### Style notes

Vocal phrasing in kan ha diskan uses **overlapping** lines — lead and response staggered — requiring rhythmic tightness unlike solo sean-nós rubato. Dancers may pause during extended song sets.

## Ornament and phrasing

Breton instrumental ornament on **bombard** and **binou** emphasises **articulation** — tongued cuts, repeated pulse notes, unison doubling rather than Irish fiddle roll density. Accordion ornament uses bellows reversals and treble fills; fiddle adopts **dance-length** bow strokes.

**Tempo negotiation:** Leaders watch the floor, not the clock. **Repeat structures:** Fest-noz sets repeat until the caller releases dancers — unlike fixed three-tune Irish session sets.

**Mode and key:** B-flat centre for sonerion pairs; D and G common for fiddle bands; accordion layouts determine practical keys for plinn nights.

## Suite Sudarmoricaine (form preview)

The **Suite Sudarmoricaine** ("South Morbihan suite") aggregates **gavotte** and related dance tunes from **Cornouaille** / south Morbihan — a reference set for **bagad** competitions and fest-noz veterans. Lesson 8 names individual tunes and recordings; here, treat it as **form bundle**: multiple gavottes chained for regional identity, not a single abstract melody.

[[entity:suite-sudarmoricaine]] appears in bagad programmes and tune books as Cornouaille shorthand.

## Reading and listening list

- **Sonerion** tune books — gavotte and plinn sections with Breton titles.
- **Bagad Bro Kemperle** — gavotte suite arrangements.
- **Yann-Fanch Kemener** — kan ha diskan and song-dance overlap.
- Wikipedia *Gavotte (folk dance)* and *Music of Brittany* — cross-check with Dastum recordings.

## Key points

- Gavotte names Cornouaille fest-noz core; Suite Sudarmoricaine bundles regional gavotte identity.
- Plinn demands steady walking pulse — especially accordion-led in Vannetais.
- Mazurka and son extend fest-noz programmes beyond gavotte alone.
- Kan ha diskan is rhythmic dialogue song, programmed beside instrumentals.
- Ornament and set length follow dancers, not session-style three-tune chains.
