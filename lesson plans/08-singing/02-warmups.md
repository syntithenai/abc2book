# Vocal Warm-Up Routines

**Track:** singing | **Difficulty:** 1/10 | **Time:** ~10 min

## Overview

Warming up prepares vocal folds, coordinates breath and tone, and prevents injury — especially before loud sessions or long gigs.

## Progressive sequence

1. Gentle hum — comfortable range, slide slowly. 2. Lip trill — connects breath to sound without throat effort. 3. Ng–ah — tongue release, forward tone. 4. Vowel pulses — short 'oo' bursts then sustain. 5. Octave glide — map chest-to-head transition.

## Hum wake-up

Soft closed-mouth hum, barely above whisper. Buzz into imaginary mug. If scratchy, stay smaller and quieter.

## Lip trill

Flutter lips while phonating. Lighten air if sputtering — smaller range, not harder blowing.

## Ng–ah

Tongue tip behind top teeth on 'ng'; open to 'ah' without wide jaw drop. Forward, clear tone for consonant-heavy lyrics.

## Safety

Stop if hoarse or tight. Warm-ups should feel easier, not harder, as you proceed.

## Quiz Questions

### Q1. Lip trills help:

- A) Connect breath to sound with minimal throat tension
- B) Increase volume only
- C) Replace all singing
- D) Tune instruments

**Answer:** A

### Q2. Warm-ups should progress from gentle to more demanding.

**Answer:** True

### Q3. If throat feels tight during warmup:

- A) Stop and reduce effort
- B) Push harder
- C) Whisper loudly
- D) Skip all future singing

**Answer:** A
