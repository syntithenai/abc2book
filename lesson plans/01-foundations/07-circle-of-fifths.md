# The Circle of Fifths and Key Signatures

**Track:** foundations | **Difficulty:** 3/10 | **Time:** ~12 min
**Prerequisites:** Major Scales, Accidentals
**Tags:** keys, circle-of-fifths, signatures

## Overview

The circle of fifths arranges all 12 major and 12 minor keys in a clock face. Clockwise motion adds sharps; counter-clockwise adds flats. Keys a fifth apart share six of seven scale tones — the smoothest modulations travel by fifths.

## Sharp keys

C (0) → G (1♯) → D (2♯) → A (3♯) → E (4♯) → B (5♯) → F♯/G♭ (6♯/6♭). Order of sharps: **F♯ C♯ G♯ D♯ A♯ E♯ B♯** — mnemonic: *Father Charles Goes Down And Ends Battle*. Last sharp + semitone up = major key.

## Flat keys

C → F (1♭) → B♭ (2♭) → E♭ (3♭) → A♭ (4♭) → D♭ (5♭) → G♭ (6♭). Order of flats reverses sharps: **B♭ E♭ A♭ D♭ G♭ C♭ F♭** — *Battle Ends And Down Goes Charles' Father*. Second-to-last flat names the major key.

## Relative minors

Each major key's relative minor shares its key signature, found a minor third below: A minor ↔ C major, E minor ↔ G major, B minor ↔ D major.

## In practice

G, D, A, E dominate sharp-side trad repertoire. B♭ and F appear on flute and box. Map tunes onto the circle to predict friendly modulations and prepare accidentals before the downbeat.

## Quiz Questions

### Q1. Order of sharps in key signatures:

- A) F♯ C♯ G♯ D♯ A♯ E♯ B♯
- B) G♯ D♯ A♯ E♯ B♯ F♯ C♯
- C) C♯ G♯ D♯ A♯ E♯ B♯ F♯
- D) F♯ G♯ A♯ B♯ C♯ D♯ E♯

**Answer:** A

### Q2. How many sharps in D major?

- A) 2 (F♯ and C♯)
- B) 1
- C) 3
- D) None

**Answer:** A

### Q3. E minor is the relative minor of G major.

**Answer:** True
