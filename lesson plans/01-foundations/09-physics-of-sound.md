# Physics of Sound and Tuning

**Track:** foundations | **Difficulty:** 3/10 | **Time:** ~10 min
**Tags:** acoustics, tuning, frequency

## Overview

Music theory rests on physical properties of sound: vibration frequency determines pitch; waveform shape determines timbre; amplitude determines loudness. Understanding acoustics explains why instruments sound different and why tuning systems matter.

## Frequency and pitch

Pitch corresponds to fundamental frequency measured in Hertz (Hz). A4 = 440 Hz is the international standard. Each octave doubles frequency: A3 = 220 Hz, A5 = 880 Hz. The human hearing range is roughly 20–20,000 Hz.

## Harmonics and timbre

Instruments produce a fundamental plus overtones (harmonics) at integer multiples. A violin's bright tone has strong upper harmonics; a flute emphasises the fundamental. Timbre — the 'colour' of sound — is the harmonic fingerprint.

## Tuning systems

**Equal temperament** divides the octave into 12 equal semitones — standard for piano and guitar. **Just intonation** uses pure frequency ratios (5:4 for major third) — sweeter for chords but limits key changes. **Pythagorean** tuning favours perfect fifths. Fiddle players often adjust intonation contextually.

## Beats and consonance

When two frequencies are slightly apart, beating occurs — pulsing loudness. Pure intervals minimise beats. Dissonance often involves close but non-aligned frequencies creating roughness.
