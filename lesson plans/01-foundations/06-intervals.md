# Intervals: Measuring Distance Between Notes

**Track:** foundations | **Difficulty:** 2/10 | **Time:** ~12 min
**Prerequisites:** Major Scales
**Tags:** intervals, ear-training, harmony

## Overview

An interval is the distance between two pitches, named by counting letter names inclusively and qualifying the size (major, minor, perfect, augmented, diminished). Intervals are the vocabulary of melody, harmony, and transposition.

## Naming intervals

Count letter names from lower to higher: C to G = fifth (C-D-E-F-G). C to E = third. Quality depends on semitone count: major third = 4 semitones; minor third = 3. Perfect fifth = 7 semitones. Augmented intervals are one semitone wider than major/perfect; diminished are one narrower.

## Consonance and dissonance

Perfect intervals (unison, fourth, fifth, octave) sound open and stable. Major and minor thirds and sixths are consonant in tonal music. Seconds and sevenths create tension. The tritone (augmented fourth / diminished fifth) was called *diabolus in musica* in medieval theory — maximally tense.

## Melodic vs harmonic

Melodic intervals occur between consecutive notes in a melody. Harmonic intervals sound simultaneously as chords. The same interval name applies; context changes perception.

## In practice

Recognising thirds and fifths by ear is the highest-leverage skill for session harmonisation. Fiddle players often narrow major thirds slightly (Pythagorean or just intonation) compared to equal temperament.

## Quiz Questions

### Q1. C to E is what interval?

- A) Major third
- B) Minor third
- C) Perfect fourth
- D) Major second

**Answer:** A — C-D-E counts a third; four semitones = major.

### Q2. Perfect intervals include:

- A) Unison, fourth, fifth, octave
- B) Major and minor thirds only
- C) All seconds and sevenths
- D) Only the octave

**Answer:** A

### Q3. A tritone spans six semitones.

**Answer:** True — Augmented fourth or diminished fifth; three whole steps.
