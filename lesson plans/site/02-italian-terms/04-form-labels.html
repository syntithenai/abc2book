Form Labels: D.C., D.S., Coda, and Fine

Track: italian-terms | Difficulty: 2/10 | Time: ~8 min

Overview

Italian navigation signs tell performers how to traverse repeats and sectional jumps in longer pieces.

Key terms

Da capo (D.C.) — from the head (beginning). D.C. al fine — return to start, play until fine (end). Dal segno (D.S.) — from the 𝄋 sign. D.S. al coda — play to ‘To Coda’, jump to coda section. Coda — concluding passage reached by jump. Fine — end point.

Practical use

Simple dance tunes use first/second endings (1. / 2.) instead. O’Carolan arrangements, classical transcriptions, and céilí set parts routinely use D.C. and D.S. Reading them keeps the ensemble aligned on repeats.

Quiz Questions

Q1. D.C. al fine means:

  • A) Return to beginning, play until fine
  • B) Skip to coda
  • C) Second ending only
  • D) Repeat last four bars

Answer: A

Q2. D.S. resumes from the segno sign, not the beginning.

Answer: True
