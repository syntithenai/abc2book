Articulation Marks: Legato, Staccato, and Accents

Track: italian-terms | Difficulty: 2/10 | Time: ~8 min

Overview

Articulation defines how notes connect or separate — as important as pitch and rhythm for expressive performance.

Core markings

Legato — smooth, connected. Staccato (dot) — detached, separated. Slur — curved line; play notes in one breath/bow without re-attacking. Tenuto (—) — full value, slight emphasis. Marcato (^) — marked, emphatic attack.

Ornaments

Trill (tr) — rapid alternation with upper note. Mordent — quick neighbour-tone flick. Turn (∞) — encircle the main note. Irish ornamentation (cuts, rolls, cran) is often learned by ear rather than from symbols.

Instrument-specific

String players articulate primarily through bowing: same-note re-bowing vs slurring changes dance feel entirely. Wind players use tonguing syllables (doo, too, doo-goo). Match articulation to dance type — crisp for reels, more legato for airs.

Quiz Questions

Q1. Legato means:

  • A) Smooth and connected
  • B) Short and detached
  • C) Very loud
  • D) Gradually slower

Answer: A

Q2. A slur indicates connected notes without re-articulation.

Answer: True
