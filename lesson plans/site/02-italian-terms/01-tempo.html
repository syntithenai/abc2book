Italian Tempo Markings

Track: italian-terms | Difficulty: 1/10 | Time: ~8 min

Overview

Italian tempo markings appear on scores worldwide, describing both speed and character. They originated when Italian composers dominated European court music in the 17th–18th centuries.

Common tempi

Largo — very slow, broad. Adagio — slow, stately. Andante — walking pace (~76–108 BPM). Moderato — moderate. Allegretto — moderately fast, lighter than allegro. Allegro — fast and lively (~120–168 BPM). Vivace — brisk and spirited. Presto — very fast (~168–200 BPM). Prestissimo — as fast as possible.

Modifiers

Molto — very. Poco — a little. Meno — less. Più — more. Non troppo — not too much. Allegro ma non troppo = fast but not excessively so. Rubato — flexible tempo within pulse.

Metronome marks

Modern scores add exact BPM: ♩ = 120. Italian words remain for character; numbers specify precision. Trad sessions negotiate tempo by ear — the leader’s first phrase sets the speed.

Quiz Questions

Q1. Which marking suggests walking pace?

  • A) Andante
  • B) Presto
  • C) Allegro
  • D) Vivace

Answer: A

Q2. Presto indicates very fast tempo.

Answer: True

Q3. Rubato means:

  • A) Flexible timing within an underlying pulse
  • B) Strict metronomic playing
  • C) Playing only half the notes
  • D) Changing key

Answer: A
