Triad Inversions and Bass Notes

Track: chords | Difficulty: 3/10 | Time: ~10 min

Overview

Inversions rearrange which chord tone sits in the bass without changing the chord’s identity.

Three positions

Root position: root in bass (C-E-G). First inversion: third in bass, written C/E. Second inversion: fifth in bass, written C/G. The chord symbol reflects root and quality; slash notation shows bass note.

Voice leading

Inversions create smoother bass lines: C → G/B → Am → G/C descends stepwise. Figured bass uses 6 (first inversion) and 6/4 (second inversion) in classical sources.

Session use

Choose inversions by ear for smooth bass motion and to keep the melody clear of the accompaniment register.

Quiz Questions

Q1. C/E is which inversion?

  • A) First inversion
  • B) Root position
  • C) Second inversion
  • D) Not C major

Answer: A

Q2. Inversions change letter name but not chord identity.

Answer: True
