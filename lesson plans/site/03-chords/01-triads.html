Triads: Major, Minor, Diminished, and Augmented

Track: chords | Difficulty: 2/10 | Time: ~10 min

Overview

A triad stacks two thirds above a root, forming the basic harmonic unit of Western music. Most folk accompaniment uses major and minor triads exclusively.

Building triads

Major: root + major 3rd + perfect 5th (C-E-G). Minor: root + minor 3rd + perfect 5th (A-C-E). Diminished: root + minor 3rd + diminished 5th (B-D-F) — tense, unstable. Augmented: root + major 3rd + augmented 5th (C-E-G♯) — rare, passing colour.

Roman numerals

In major keys: I, ii, iii, IV, V, vi, vii°. Capital = major; lowercase = minor; ° = diminished. Matching a tune’s key to its diatonic triads is the first step toward session backup.

In practice

On guitar or piano, learn triad shapes in G, D, A, C, and Em. Three notes define the chord quality; doublings and voicing are flexible.

Quiz Questions

Q1. C major triad notes:

  • A) C-E-G
  • B) C-E♭-G
  • C) C-F-A
  • D) C-D-G

Answer: A

Q2. Minor triad differs from major by a smaller third above root.

Answer: True
