Major Scales — Applied

Track: theory | Tier: applied | Difficulty: 3/10 | Time: ~30 min Prerequisites: Major Scales — Introduction Tags: scales, major, keys, circle-of-fifths

Overview

Applied tier: construct any major scale, read key signatures, and relate scales to practical keys on your instrument. Revisit WWHWWWH from the intro tier — here you use it for transposition and sight-reading.

Key signatures and the circle of fifths

Each sharp key adds one sharp in order: F♯, C♯, G♯, D♯, A♯, E♯, B♯.

Each flat key adds one flat: B♭, E♭, A♭, D♭, G♭, C♭, F♭.

Moving clockwise on the circle of fifths raises the key by a fifth (adds a sharp). Counter-clockwise adds a flat.

Tetrachords

A major scale splits into two tetrachords (W-W-H each). Upper tetrachord of C (G-A-B-C) mirrors the lower (C-D-E-F). Splitting scales this way speeds mental transposition: learn the tetrachord shapes, then join them at the tonic.

Practical keys

Fiddle, whistle, and guitar session repertoire clusters in G, D, A, C, E major (and related modes). Drill scales in these keys until fingerings are automatic before adding remote signatures.

Spiral link

Intervals (next unit) measure the distances you have been counting as whole and half steps. Cadences (later) explain why degree 5 and degree 7 feel like “going home.”
