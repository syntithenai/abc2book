Major Scales — Advanced

Track: theory | Tier: advanced | Difficulty: 6/10 | Time: ~35 min Prerequisites: Major Scales — Applied Tags: scales, modulation, tetrachords, analysis

Overview

Advanced tier: treat major scales as movable systems — modal mixture, brief tonicisation, and score analysis. You should already fluently build any major scale (applied tier) before this lesson.

Modal mixture and borrowed colour

Major keys sometimes borrow ♭VI, ♭VII, ♭III from the parallel minor for colour (e.g. in folk and rock). Recognising these as scale-degree alterations rather than “wrong notes” is an analysis skill built on secure major-scale degree naming.

Tonicisation vs modulation

Tonicisation: temporary emphasis of a non-tonic chord (often via secondary dominant — see harmony track).

Modulation: established change of tonic. Major scales provide the target collection for the new key. Ear training: follow the pivot chord or sudden new accidental set.

Scale networks

Relative minor shares the same key signature (A minor ↔ C major). Parallel minor shares the same tonic (C minor ↔ C major). Advanced musicians flip between these views when arranging or improvising.

Representative depth (not exhaustive)

Know the major scale as a system — not every historical variant (musical modes, maqam, rag) which belong to regional units. This lesson closes the major-scale thread; modes get their own spiral in foundations and in Celtic/Irish regional study.
