Major Scales — Introduction

Track: theory | Tier: intro | Difficulty: 1/10 | Time: ~20 min Prerequisites: none Tags: scales, major, foundations

Overview

The major scale is the foundational pitch collection of Western music and of much folk and session repertoire. This tier names the pattern, scale degrees, and the leading tone — concepts you will revisit in every later harmony and ear-training lesson.

The WWHWWWH pattern

A major scale follows whole–whole–half–whole–whole–whole–half between consecutive degrees.

From C: C D E F G A B C — no accidentals.

From G: one sharp (F♯). From F: one flat (B♭). The pattern is invariant; only the starting note and key signature change.

Scale degree names

Degree Name Role
1 tonic home
5 dominant tension toward tonic
7 leading tone semitone below tonic; pulls upward
8 octave return

A phrase ending on scale degree 7 feels unresolved — you will hear this again in cadence lessons.

First hearing goal

Sing or play one major scale in C, G, and D. Name the intervals as whole or half steps between adjacent notes.

Quiz Questions

Q1. Major scale interval pattern:

  • A) WWHWWWH
  • B) WHWWHWW
  • C) WWWHWWH
  • D) WHWWWHW

Answer: A

Q2. The seventh degree in major is called the leading tone.

Answer: True

Q3. G major has one sharp: F♯.

Answer: True

Q4. Scale degree 1 is called:

  • A) Tonic
  • B) Dominant
  • C) Mediant
  • D) Subdominant

Answer: A

Q5. A major scale from F requires one flat.

Answer: True

Q6. The distance from B to C in C major is:

  • A) Half step
  • B) Whole step
  • C) Minor third
  • D) Perfect fourth

Answer: A

Q7. Octave is scale degree 8.

Answer: True

Q8. Session keys often include G and D major.

Answer: True

Q9. WWHWWWH means two whole steps, then a half step, then three whole steps, then a half step.

Answer: True

Q10. Leading tone sits a semitone below the tonic.

Answer: True

Q11. C major uses only white keys on piano.

Answer: True

Q12. Changing the starting note while keeping WWHWWWH changes the key but not the major quality.

Answer: True
