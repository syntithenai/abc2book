Flute and Tin Whistle

Track: instruments | Difficulty: 2/10 | Time: ~12 min

Overview

The flute is an aerophone — sound from air splitting across an edge or hole. Concert flute (Boehm system, 19th century) is metal with complex keywork. Tin whistle (penny whistle) is a simple six-hole fipple flute central to Irish trad.

Concert flute

C instrument, range roughly C4–C7. Silver, gold, or nickel alloy. Embouchure (lip shape) controls tone. Baroque flute was wooden, one key.

Tin whistle

Typically D major (most common session key) but available in many keys. Diatonic with cross-fingerings for accidentals. Clarke, Generation, and Feadóg are classic brands. Simple enough for beginners; expressive in expert hands (Mary Bergin, Joanie Madden).

Wooden flute

Irish traditional wooden flute (concert pitch) — breathy, reedy tone. Keyless or keyed. 8-key and 4-key systems common.

Quiz Questions

Q1. Tin whistle is a:

  • A) Fipple flute (aerophone)
  • B) Bowed string
  • C) Struck percussion
  • D) Keyboard

Answer: A

Q2. Most common session whistle key:

  • A) D
  • B) B♭
  • C) F♯
  • D) A♭

Answer: A

Q3. Boehm system improved flute keywork in the 19th century.

Answer: True
