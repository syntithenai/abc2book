Uilleann Pipes

Track: instruments | Difficulty: 3/10 | Time: ~12 min

Overview

The uilleann pipes (IPA: ˈɪlən) are the characteristic bagpipe of Ireland — softer and more chromatic than Highland pipes. ‘Uilleann’ is Irish for ‘elbow,’ referring to bellows pumped by the player’s arm.

Components

Bellows (elbow-driven, not mouth-blown). Bag stores air. Chanter: conical bore, two octaves, closed end — stopped notes enable staccato. Drones (typically three) and regulators (keyed pipes for chords) optional.

Chromatic capability

Half-holing and cross-fingerings give a chromatic chanter — unlike many mouth-blown pipes limited to mixolydian scale. Concert pitch, usually D.

History

Developed 18th–19th century from earlier Irish bellows pipes. Revival led by Seamus Ennis, Leo Rowsome. Liam O’Flynn brought pipes to international concert stages.

Quiz Questions

Q1. Uilleann pipes are inflated by:

  • A) Elbow bellows
  • B) Mouth blowing only
  • C) Electricity
  • D) Foot pump exclusively

Answer: A

Q2. Uilleann chanter can play staccato due to closed end.

Answer: True

Q3. Regulators on uilleann pipes:

  • A) Keyed pipes for harmonic accompaniment
  • B) Volume controls
  • C) Tuning pegs only
  • D) Drum sticks

Answer: A
