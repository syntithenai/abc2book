J.S. Bach: Counterpoint and Dance Forms

Track: history | Difficulty: 3/10 | Time: ~10 min

Overview

Johann Sebastian Bach (1685–1750) worked in German churches and courts, composing fugues, chorales, suites, and partitas central to Western music education.

Key works

The Well-Tempered Clavier (48 preludes and fugues in all keys) demonstrated equal-temperament keyboard writing. Brandenburg Concertos showcase Baroque orchestration. Cello Suites and violin Partitas remain performance staples.

Dance forms

Bach absorbed allemandes, courantes, sarabandes, and gigues into instrumental suites — linking court music to European dance tradition.

Legacy for trad

Two-part invention shows bass implying harmony while melody sings — same split session guitarists use. Ornament tables influence baroque-influenced flute and harp gracing.
