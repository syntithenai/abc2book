Irish Instruments — Rhythm and Accompaniment

Track: regions | Tier: 3 | Difficulty: 4/10 Prerequisites: regions-celtic-ireland-02-instruments-melody Tags: ireland, bodhrán, guitar, bouzouki, banjo, mandolin, concertina, accordion

Overview

Not every trad instrument carries the tune. Bodhrán, guitar, bouzouki, mandolin, banjo, concertina, accordion, bones, and harmonica often provide rhythm, harmony, or chordal backing. Good accompaniment supports the melody without dominating it.

Bodhrán and bones

The bodhrán is a single-headed frame drum, struck with a tipper (cipín) or the hand. The player controls pitch and timbre by pressing the hand inside the skin. It became a central session instrument in the revival era — used sparingly and tastefully in many circles.

Bones (paired wooden or bone sticks) click out rhythm in a tradition shared with Appalachian music. They are portable and loud for their size.

Session role: Pulse and lift — must follow the melody’s phrasing, not dictate it.

Guitar

Guitar arrived in Irish trad later than native instruments but is now standard in sessions and bands. Players use DADGAD or standard tuning for modal accompaniment, capoing for key changes. Strumming patterns follow reel and jig pulse.

Session role: Chordal backing and rhythmic drive; avoid competing with melody ornaments.

Irish bouzouki and mandolin

The Irish bouzouki (adapted from Greek bouzouki in the 1960s–70s) has long scale length and pairs of strings — ideal for open-chord accompaniment and counter-melody. Mandolin adds bright, percussive chord chops and occasional melody doubles.

Both suit D, G, and related session keys. Andy Irvine and others popularised bouzouki in planxty-era bands.

Session role: Harmonic bed, rhythmic “chop,” and melodic fills between phrases.

Four-string banjo and tenor banjo

Irish trad often uses the four-string tenor banjo (not bluegrass five-string) tuned in fifths (commonly C-G-D-A or G-D-A-E), played with a plectrum. It cuts through sessions with a sharp, driving attack on reels.

Some players use mandolin-banjo hybrids; the 4-string tenor is the session norm in Ireland.

Session role: Percussive melody doubling and rhythmic accent — Barney McKenna (The Dubliners) defined the style for many listeners.

Concertina and accordion

The concertina (Anglo system most common in Irish music) is a bellows-driven free-reed instrument — compact and expressive for melody or accompaniment. The button accordion (Irish “box”) dominates in many regional styles (Clare, Kerry, Newfoundland links).

Irish accordion playing uses bass chords sparingly compared to continental styles; melody and ornament lead.

Session role: Melody in box-heavy regions; chordal accompaniment in céilí bands and sessions.

Harmonica

Harmonica appears in trad and folk-rock contexts — less central than pipes or fiddle but used for melody and blues-inflected fills, especially in fusion and pub-band settings.

Session role: Occasional melody or colour; not a core session instrument in all regions.

Quiz Questions

Q1. DADGAD tuning on guitar is associated with:

  • A) Modal Irish and Celtic accompaniment
  • B) Classical violin only
  • C) Drum kit setup
  • D) Uilleann pipe regulators

Answer: A

Q2. The bodhrán is a double-headed snare drum from Scotland.

Answer: False — It is a single-headed Irish frame drum.

Q3. Irish session banjo is typically:

  • A) Four-string tenor banjo with a plectrum
  • B) Five-string bluegrass banjo only
  • C) A keyboard instrument
  • D) A brass horn

Answer: A

Q4. The Irish bouzouki was adapted into trad primarily in:

  • A) The mid–late 20th century from Greek models
  • B) Medieval Gaelic courts only
  • C) The 18th century by Bach
  • D) Electronic studios in the 2020s only

Answer: A

Q5. Anglo concertina and Irish button accordion both use:

  • A) Bellows and free reeds
  • B) Bowed horsehair strings
  • C) Plucked nylon only
  • D) Digital sampling

Answer: A

Q6. Bones are played by:

  • A) Clicking a pair of sticks held in the hand
  • B) Striking a metal cymbal with a mallet
  • C) Blowing across a fipple hole
  • D) Pressing piano keys

Answer: A

Q7. Good bodhrán playing in a session should:

  • A) Follow the melody’s phrasing and stay subtle
  • B) Always play louder than the fiddle
  • C) Ignore the tune’s rhythm entirely
  • D) Replace the melody instrument

Answer: A

Q8. Mandolin accompaniment often uses a percussive “chop” on off-beats.

Answer: True

Q9. Harmonica is the primary melody instrument in every Irish region.

Answer: False — Fiddle, flute, and whistle are far more central.

Q10. Tenor banjo tuning in Irish trad often uses intervals of:

  • A) Fifths, similar to mandolin family instruments
  • B) Only octaves with no fifths
  • C) Microtonal clusters only
  • D) Random tuning each bar

Answer: A
