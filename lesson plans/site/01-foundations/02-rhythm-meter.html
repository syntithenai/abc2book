Note Durations and Time Signatures

Track: foundations | Difficulty: 0/10 | Time: ~10 min Tags: rhythm, meter, notation

Overview

Rhythm organises music in time. Note values show how long each pitch lasts relative to the beat; time signatures group beats into measures (bars). Together they create the rhythmic grid dancers feel and musicians count.

Note values

From longest to shortest common values: whole (4 beats in 4/4), half (2 beats), quarter (1 beat), eighth (½ beat), sixteenth (¼ beat). A dot after a note adds half its value. Ties connect notes across bar lines. In ABC notation, L:1/8 sets the default unit; a2 doubles length, /2 halves it.

Simple time signatures

The top number counts beats per bar; the bottom shows the beat unit. 4/4 (common time): four quarter-note beats — reels, marches, most songs. 2/4: two quarter beats — polkas, some hornpipes. 3/4: three quarter beats — waltzes. 2/2 (cut time): two half-note beats — fast reels feel driven in two.

Compound time signatures

6/8: two beats per bar, each divided into three eighth notes — the jig pulse (“JIG-ged-y JIG-ged-y”). 9/8: three beats, each a triplet — slip jigs. 12/8: four beats of triplets — blues shuffle, some slow airs. Compound meter groups beats in threes while the top number is divisible by three.

Syncopation and pick-up notes

Syncopation stresses weak beats or offbeats, creating forward drive in jazz, reggae, and swung hornpipes. Pick-up (anacrusis) notes begin a phrase before the first full bar — common in reels where the melody starts on beat four of the previous imaginary bar.

In practice

Tap your foot to the main beat, not every subdivision. In 6/8, feel two beats; in 4/4, feel four. Matching foot-tap to written meter is the fastest fix for sight-reading confusion.

Quiz Questions

Q1. What does 6/8 time most strongly suggest?

  • A) Two beats per bar, each divided into three eighth notes
  • B) Six equal stressed beats
  • C) Three quarter-note beats (waltz)
  • D) Four beats with backbeat

Answer: A — 6/8 is compound duple: two main beats, each a triplet.

Q2. In 4/4 time, how many quarter-note beats are in one bar?

  • A) 4
  • B) 2
  • C) 3
  • D) 8

Answer: A — The top number of simple time signatures gives beats per measure.

Q3. A dotted quarter note in 4/4 lasts how many beats?

  • A) 1.5
  • B) 2
  • C) 0.75
  • D) 3

Answer: A — A dot adds half the note’s value: 1 + ½ = 1.5 beats.

Q4. Cut time (2/2) is often used for fast reels.

Answer: True — 2/2 emphasises a two-beat feel at brisk tempo.
