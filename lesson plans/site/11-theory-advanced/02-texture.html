Texture: Monophony to Polyphony

Track: theory-advanced | Difficulty: 3/10 | Time: ~12 min

Overview

Texture describes how many melodic lines sound simultaneously and their relationship.

Monophony

Single melodic line — unison singing, solo whistle, plainchant. No harmony.

Homophony

Melody with chordal accompaniment — most pop, classical melody+accompaniment, hymnody.

Polyphony

Multiple independent melodies — fugue, round, session twin-fiddle countermelody.

Heterophony

Same melody simultaneously with slight variations — common in non-Western traditions and informal group singing.

Density

Thin (solo) vs thick (full orchestra tutti). Arrangers control density for emotional arc.

Quiz Questions

Q1. Homophony features:

  • A) Melody with chordal accompaniment
  • B) Single line only
  • C) No melody at all
  • D) Random noise

Answer: A

Q2. Polyphony has multiple independent melodic lines.

Answer: True

Q3. Unison singing is:

  • A) Monophonic
  • B) Polyphonic
  • C) Homophonic
  • D) Heterophonic always

Answer: A
