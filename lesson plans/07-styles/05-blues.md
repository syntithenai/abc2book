# Blues: Form, Scale, and Expression

**Track:** styles | **Difficulty:** 4/10 | **Time:** ~10 min

## Overview

Blues combines repeating harmonic form, a distinctive scale, and microtonal inflection — foundation of jazz, rock, and R&B.

## Twelve-bar blues

I–IV–V (often dominant sevenths) over 12 bars with standard turnarounds. Variants: 8-bar, 16-bar regional styles.

## Blues scale

Minor pentatonic plus lowered 3rd, 5th, and 7th — 'blue notes' bent between piano keys. Vocal cry parallel to sean-nós inflection.

## Folk intersection

Appalachian ballads meet African American song forms. Trad bands add shuffle to hornpipes. Three-chord map for pub jams.

## Quiz Questions

### Q1. Twelve-bar blues uses:

- A) I, IV, V harmonies
- B) Only minor ii
- C) One chord throughout
- D) Diminished chains only

**Answer:** A

### Q2. Blue notes involve flattened 3rd/7th with pitch bending.

**Answer:** True
